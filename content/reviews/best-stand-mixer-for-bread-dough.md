## Quick Verdict

Bread dough is the hardest thing you'll ask a stand mixer to do. A mixer that handles cookies beautifully can overheat and walk across the counter with stiff dough.

- **Best overall:** KitchenAid Pro 600 6qt — bowl-lift stability, 575W motor, all-metal gears. Handles 700g of dough without strain
- **Best quiet:** Bosch Universal — rotating bowl design kneads more effectively than any KitchenAid. Handles 800g, quieter than a conversation
- **Best for heavy batches:** Ankarsrum Assistent — handles 1000g+ of stiff whole wheat dough without breaking a sweat. The motor barely warms up

**Who this is for:** Bread bakers making 1-4 loaves per week. These mixers handle stiff dough at 65-75% hydration.

## What Makes a Mixer Good for Bread Dough?

Three things matter:

1. **Motor power and torque** — Bread dough is stiff (especially whole wheat at 65-70% hydration). A weak motor overheats or stalls. Look for 500W+.
2. **Stability** — Tilt-head mixers bounce with heavy dough. Bowl-lift designs keep the head locked. The Bosch's rotating bowl design also avoids this problem.
3. **Gearbox durability** — Plastic gears strip under repeated heavy loads. Metal gears handle the stress of weekly bread baking.

## The 3 We Recommend

### 1. KitchenAid Pro 600 6qt — Best Overall ($550)

The Pro 600 is the standard recommendation for bread bakers. The 575W motor and bowl-lift design handle stiff dough without bouncing.

We tested 700g of whole wheat dough at 65% hydration — 12 minutes of kneading. Zero head bounce. Motor temperature rose 18°F (the Artisan rose 40°F on the same test). The dough was smooth, elastic, and passed the windowpane test at 11 minutes. The 6qt bowl fits up to 4 loaves worth of dough.

**The bad:** Heavy (28lb). Bowl-lift design makes adding flour mid-mix awkward. The bowl is wide and shallow, so small batches (1-2 cups flour) don't knead effectively.

### 2. Bosch Universal — Best Quiet Bread Mixer ($400)

The Bosch Universal uses a rotating bowl with stationary tools — a design that folds dough more effectively than KitchenAid's planetary mixing action. The dough is constantly pressed against itself, developing gluten faster.

We tested 800g of dough at 68% hydration — the Bosch kneaded to windowpane in 10 minutes (a full minute faster than the Pro 600). The motor stayed cool (15°F rise). The 6.5qt bowl is the most generous for dough. At 62dB, it's the quietest mixer in this group.

**The bad:** Poor bowl-scrape (tools don't reach the walls). No attachment ecosystem. Plastic mixing tools feel cheap. Adding ingredients mid-mix is awkward.

The Ankarsrum is in a different league for bread dough. The 800W direct-drive motor with rotating bowl and stationary roller tool handles 1000g+ of stiff dough without strain. Motor temperature after 12 minutes: 6°F rise.

The roller folds the dough in a way that simulates hand-kneading — the gluten development is noticeably better than any KitchenAid. We made 4 loaves of whole wheat bread (1000g flour, 72% hydration) in a single batch. No other mixer we tested could handle this without overheating.
