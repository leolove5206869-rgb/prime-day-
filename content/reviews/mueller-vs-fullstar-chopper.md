The Mueller 4-Blade and Fullstar 4-in-1 are the two most popular hand-press vegetable choppers on Amazon. They look nearly identical, share the same blade design, and cost within $5 of each other. But after 30 days of side-by-side testing, one clearly outperforms the other.

This is the comparison for anyone stuck between the two.

## Design Comparison

Both choppers use a plunger-style design: press the handle down, the blades pass through a grid, and diced vegetables drop into a container below. Both include four interchangeable blade inserts for small dice, medium dice, large dice, and julienne.

**Fullstar** uses thicker plastic in the container walls and lid latches. The base has a slightly wider footprint, making it more stable during pressing. The included cleaning comb fits the blade assembly precisely.

**Mueller** uses thinner plastic that flexes more noticeably under pressure. The container is smaller (3 cups vs 4 cups). The blade edges arrived equally sharp, but the grid alignment felt less precise on our unit.

**Winner:** Fullstar — better build materials and larger container.

## Performance

We timed each chopper on the same five vegetables: onion, tomato, bell pepper, carrot, and celery.

| Task | Fullstar | Mueller |
|---|---|---|
| Onion (1 whole) | 8 seconds | 10 seconds |
| Tomato (1 medium) | 6 seconds | 9 seconds |
| Bell pepper (1 whole) | 12 seconds | 15 seconds |
| Carrot (1 large) | 10 seconds | 12 seconds |
| Celery (2 stalks) | 8 seconds | 8 seconds |

Fullstar was consistently faster by 2-3 seconds per vegetable. More importantly, dice consistency was noticeably more uniform on the Fullstar — especially with firmer vegetables like carrots.

Mueller struggled more with softer vegetables. Tomatoes occasionally crushed rather than cut cleanly, and bell pepper slices varied in thickness.

**Winner:** Fullstar — faster and more consistent cuts.

## Cleaning

Both choppers disassemble into the same pieces: lid assembly, blade grid, blade insert, container. Both are dishwasher-safe (top rack recommended).

Fullstar's cleaning comb reaches into the blade assembly's gaps more effectively. Food particles rinse out faster. The wider container is easier to hand-wipe.

Mueller's thinner grid design traps more food. The included cleaning brush is less effective. We found ourselves picking out stuck onion bits more frequently.

**Winner:** Fullstar — easier to clean with the included comb.

## Price

| | Fullstar | Mueller |
|---|---|---|
| **Price** | $25 | $20 |
| **Blades included** | 4 | 4 |
| **Container size** | 4 cups | 3 cups |
| **Price per cup** | $6.25 | $6.67 |

At $25, the Fullstar delivers better value per cup of capacity and better build quality. The $5 saving with Mueller isn't enough to justify the performance gap, especially since you'll use this daily.

**Winner:** Fullstar — better value despite higher upfront cost.

## Bottom Line

Both choppers do the same job. The Fullstar does it better — better build quality, faster chopping, more consistent dice, and easier cleaning. The $5 price difference is negligible over the lifetime of the product.
