Toaster vs Toaster Oven — Which Should You Buy?

June 9, 2026 · 3 min read

Quick Verdict

Buy a slot toaster if you primarily make toast, bagels, and frozen waffles — it’s faster, more compact, and produces better toast than any toaster oven. Buy a toaster oven if you also reheat pizza, roast vegetables, bake small items, or want an air fryer — the versatility makes it worth the counter space.

Most people should own a toaster oven. The slot toaster’s one-trick nature is hard to justify for a dedicated appliance. Here’s why.

What a Slot Toaster Does Best

A slot toaster is an optimized single-purpose machine. It’s engineered for one job: browning bread quickly and evenly.

Pros:

  • Faster toasting (2-3 minutes vs 4-6 minutes in a toaster oven)
  • More even browning — dedicated heating elements and self-centering racks produce consistent results
  • Smaller footprint (takes 1/3 the counter space of a toaster oven)
  • Better bagel function — toasts cut side while warming skin
  • Lighter (2-7 lb vs 10-25 lb), easier to store and move
  • Cheaper ($30-200 vs $50-300)
  • No preheat needed — push lever and go

Cons:

  • Only toasts — can’t bake, broil, roast, or reheat
  • Limited to bread-shaped items (no pizza, no cookies, no vegetables)
  • 2-4 slots mean batch cooking for more people
  • Can’t cook frozen food from frozen (waffles work, chicken nuggets don’t)
  • Uneven on uneven bread (croissants, thick rolls won’t fit)

Best for: Toast-for-breakfast households, small kitchens, anyone who eats bread products and nothing else from a countertop appliance. See our best toaster guide for top picks.

What a Toaster Oven Does Better

A toaster oven is a miniature convection oven. It does everything a slot toaster does — plus baking, broiling, roasting, reheating, and often air frying.

Pros:

  • Versatile — bake cookies, roast chicken thighs, reheat pizza, toast bread
  • Larger capacity — fits a 12-inch pizza, 8 chicken wings, 4-6 slices of toast
  • Air fry function in most modern models ($100+)
  • Can cook frozen food directly (fries, chicken nuggets, fish sticks)
  • Handles irregular shapes (croissants, rolls, pita)
  • Often replaces a full oven for daily cooking

Cons:

  • Toasting takes longer (4-6 minutes) and is less even than a slot toaster
  • Takes more counter space (13-19 inches deep vs 8-12 inches)
  • Heavy (10-25 lb) — not portable, harder to store
  • Requires preheat for most foods
  • Crumb tray is smaller, cleaning is more involved
  • More expensive for good performance ($100+)

Best for: People who cook — anyone who reheats leftovers, bakes small items, or wants to avoid turning on their full oven. See our best toaster oven guide for top picks.

Bottom Line

Buy a slot toaster (Breville Die-Cast or Cuisinart CPT-122) — if you make toast daily and rarely use a countertop oven for anything else. The toast quality is better, the space is smaller, and the cost is lower.

Buy a toaster oven (Breville Smart Oven or Cuisinart Chef’s Convection) — if your idea of breakfast includes reheated pizza, roasted vegetables, or anything that isn’t sliced bread.

The data is clear: a toaster oven does everything a slot toaster does, plus much more. The only real reasons to buy a slot toaster are absolute toast quality (slot toasters toast more evenly), counter space constraints (a toaster is 1/3 the footprint), and budget ($40 buys a great slot toaster, $140 buys a good toaster oven).
