Handheld vs Electric Milk Frother — Which One Should You Buy?

3 min read

Quick Verdict

If you make 1-2 milk drinks per week, get a handheld frother ($12-18). If you make daily lattes or cappuccinos, get an automatic electric frother ($40-100). The automatic produces better microfoam with zero effort. The handheld is cheaper, smaller, and more versatile.

Froth Quality

Winner: Automatic

Automatic frothers (Breville, Secura) use induction heating with magnetic stirring. The milk is heated to 150°F while being spun at consistent speed. Result: dense microfoam with bubbles < 0.5mm that holds shape for 10+ minutes. This is indistinguishable from espresso machine steam wand foam.

Handheld frothers (Zulay, PowerLix) spin a spring whisk at high RPM. You control the angle and depth, so results depend on technique. With practice you get decent foam, but bubbles are larger (1-2mm) and collapse after 3-5 minutes. Cold foam is better than hot foam with handhelds.

Ease of Use

Winner: Automatic

Automatic: pour milk to fill line, press button, walk away. 90 seconds later, perfect hot foam. One setting for thick foam, another for light foam, another for cold. Zero attention required.

Handheld: fill a tall mug with cold milk, heat in microwave (or use cold), submerge whisk, press button, tilt and move wand in a circular motion for 20-30 seconds. Requires attention and some practice. Easy to over-froth or splatter.

Cleaning

Winner: Handheld

Handheld: rinse the spring whisk under hot water for 5 seconds. Done. No milk residue sticks if you rinse immediately.

Automatic: wipe the interior with a damp cloth. The nonstick coating usually releases milk easily, but the heating element at the base can develop burnt milk smell over time. Most automatic jugs are not fully dishwasher safe. The Breville’s jug is dishwasher safe, which puts it ahead of most.

Versatility

Winner: Handheld

Handheld frothers work with any container, any milk quantity (as little as 2oz), and any milk temperature. They also froth matcha, protein shakes, eggs, and even sauces.

Automatic frothers have a minimum fill line (4-7oz) and only work with their specific carafe. They do heat milk, which handhelds can’t, but cannot froth non-dairy items or very small quantities.

Price

Winner: Handheld

Handheld frothers cost $10-18. Automatic frothers cost $35-100. A handheld pays for itself after 2-3 home lattes versus cafe prices. An automatic pays for itself after 15-20.

Bottom Line
